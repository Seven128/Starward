## Cross-Control And State Invariants

- `sky-orientation-canvas` is a pointing window into the sky, not a whole-hemisphere planisphere. For the inherited observing coordinates and committed time, real star and target directions use one three-dimensional view transform and one explicitly defined angular field of view with the actual canvas aspect ratio. The phone's back-facing direction determines the view center; pitch, yaw and roll change that view basis rather than translating a compressed circular sky. Angular separation, handedness and aspect ratio must remain geometrically consistent; off-frustum and behind-view objects are not clamped into apparent on-screen star positions. The panel's static 2D overview may retain its distinct overview projection and must not define the immersive projection.
  On-site comparison with the naked-eye sky is meaningful only when the observing coordinates/time match the observer's location/time. Remote cloud observation continues to use the selected formal spot and shared time; this requirement does not silently switch to phone GPS or introduce a second location/time owner. Exact apparent 1:1 angular size depends on physical screen size and viewing distance and cannot be claimed from logical pixels alone. A declared field of view and measured pointing accuracy are separate requirements: sensor reference frame, compass north reference, calibration, sample freshness and platform differences must be established rather than hidden by visual offsets. Geometry tests cover cardinal directions, pitch/roll, view boundaries and viewport aspect ratios; real-device comparison must establish pointing correspondence and disclose remaining uncertainty. Simulated pose, a visually pleasing screenshot or catalog presence alone does not establish alignment. This is a rendered star chart and does not imply camera AR permission or imagery.
